# Phase 2: Scope

Goal: one named user making one decision and taking one action, with a written cut list and a feature-stop time. "A small problem solved well beats a big one solved vaguely." "One feature that runs beats five that almost do."

## One user, one decision, one action
Write three lines and keep them visible:
- **User:** a specific person in a specific situation ("a housing-society committee member who just got a municipal notice").
- **Decision:** what they must decide ("who should fix this, and what exactly was promised?").
- **Action:** what they do in the product, ending in the real world (a letter sent, a list printed, a booking made).

Then write the **outcome chain**: product use → action → real-world change → the track's core outcome. Check that every link in the chain is plausible and that none of it is overclaimed. The track's core outcome is the hero; put its key phrase in the first 15 s of video and the first line of the README.

## Choosing the lead user when there are several
Score each candidate. Do not trust the first argument; the impact chain alone picks the wrong user.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Can a judge *be* this user and try it in 10 seconds? | "Usable without a walkthrough" is usually scored |
| Does the team have a lived story with this user? | Real stories beat personas in the video |
| Is real use possible before the deadline? | Real users are rare among winners, so they stand out |
| How short is the chain from use to the real-world outcome? | Impact score |
| Does it match the organiser's own example users? | Shows you read the brief |
| Does the tagline fit this user? | Story coherence |

Lead with one user. Give the others one line each in the video ("and ward officers get a check-first list").

## Extra flows are supporting features, never the point
List every feature with the criterion it moves and its cost in hours. Keep only what moves a criterion per hour. Cut the rest into the "on hold" list with one line on why.

## "Are we too simple?"
Winners in the same series may have built smartwatch or VR entries. That is not a reason to add a gadget: the device fit their user. If the product looks simple, **make the hidden depth visible** instead: real data volume, verification, the pipeline running, honest handling of doubt. Judges penalise shoehorned features and "three features and no centre".

## Feature stop and freeze
- Set a feature-stop time (default: about 75% of the event). After it, only fixes, verification, video and submission work.
- When the user says the product is frozen, keep it frozen. Technical fixes such as responsiveness and bugs are allowed; new product behaviour is not, unless the user says "go".

## Wording rules
Write down the words the product will and will not use (for example "promised in writing" and "check first", never "violated"). Data that shows a promise, a risk or a pattern is not proof of wrongdoing. Accusing language is a legal, ethical and scoring risk.

## Done means
The three lines, the outcome chain, the cut list, the on-hold list and the feature-stop time are written in the project docs.
